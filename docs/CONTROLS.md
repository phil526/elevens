# Controls

Movement is relative to the camera: up moves away from the camera, right moves to screen-right, whichever half you are playing. In the Broadcast view the camera turns towards each end, and the controls turn with it, so up is still "into the screen". The same actions are available on every device; buttons change meaning with possession.

## Starting a match

The game opens on the main menu. **Kick off** (already highlighted: Enter, A or a tap) plays a match against the computer with your last setup, shown above it (clubs, kits, difficulty, length and the draw rule). **Change clubs or settings** opens Choose the clubs, which has its own **Kick off**; **Match settings** there shows difficulty, length and the draw rule, with tactics, formation, sliders, marking, auto-switch and quick restarts under **More options**. While a cup or league is running, **Continue** plays your next match in one press. **More modes** opens local multiplayer, cup and league, practice and tutorial, online and profile. More modes and More options stay open once opened (per device).

## Changing the controls

Main menu or pause menu → **Controls**. The **Controller** and **Keyboard** tabs list every action with two slots each; **All controls** is the full reference table, which always shows your current keys and buttons.

- Select a slot (click, tap, or highlight it with the stick/D-pad and press A), then press the new key or button. With a controller, let go of A first: the next button pressed is taken.
- A key or button already used by another action swaps: that action takes the slot's old key or button. If the slot was empty it simply moves, and the menu warns about any action left with nothing.
- Keyboard: Esc cancels, Backspace/Delete clears the slot. Esc always pauses and can't be reassigned; Ctrl, Alt and Cmd can't be used. Controller: waiting 6 seconds cancels. The left stick, D-pad and right-stick flick are fixed, and in menus A always selects and B always goes back, whatever is assigned.
- ✕ removes an action's last key or button; **Reset keyboard/controller to defaults** puts that device back.
- Changes save straight away (browser storage, with the other settings) and apply at once, mid-match too. On-screen button hints follow them. Touch controls are unchanged.

## Ball control and dribbling

- **Better dribblers keep it closer.** A player's Control rating sets how far ahead each touch goes and how tightly he turns with the ball.
- **Heavy touches.** Weaker players under pressure, sprinting or on a wet pitch sometimes take a heavy touch that runs away from them (you will see "Heavy touch"). Close control and better players avoid most of them. The difficulty's help setting makes them rarer for you.
- **First touch away from a defender.** Hold a direction as the ball arrives and the touch goes that way. A defender closing in from the other side can be turned ("Turned him with the first touch").
- **Chest and thigh control.** A ball dropping at chest height is chested down, lower ones are taken on the thigh, into your stride when you hold a direction.

## Kick power and the weaker foot

Pass, through ball, lob and shot all charge while the button is held, with the bar above your player showing the power and what will be played (PASS, DRIVEN PASS, CHIP, ...). Releasing just before the ball arrives plays it first time.

Every player has a stronger foot and a weak-foot rating from 2 to 5 stars, shown on the player card (for example "R ★★★☆☆"). The ball on the weaker side of the body, or a shot opened up well across to that side, is struck with the weaker foot: up to 10% less power and a wider spread, depending on the stars. Computer players follow the same rule.

Passes are harder in harder situations: an opponent close by, running fast, passing across or behind your body, using the weaker foot or playing it first time all widen the error. A simple short pass with time on the ball and your body open is cleaner than before. Receivers come towards a pass rather than waiting for it, and computer players half-turn to face forward as the ball arrives.

## Crosses

From wide areas of the final third the bar names the cross (FLOATED CROSS, WHIPPED CROSS, LOW CROSS). Computer teams use all three: low crosses from near the byline to a near-post runner, whipped crosses (also early) to runners, and floated balls to the far post.

## Your keeper's distribution

When your goalkeeper catches the ball, they hold it for up to 5 seconds while you choose. Aim with the stick and hold the button for distance (a tap for a near teammate, a long hold for a far one); the ring shows who it is going to and the bar names the kick.

| Button | Keyboard | Controller (default) | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pass | J | A [✕] | **Roll out** along the ground to a nearby defender or midfielder: slow and very accurate |
| Lob | K | B [○] | **Throw** overarm, up to about 40 m: quick, for starting a counter-attack down a flank |
| Through ball | O | Y [△] | **Drop-kick**: flat and fast into a forward's stride, 30-60 m |
| Shoot | L | X [□] | **Punt**: high and long, up to about 70 m, less accurate |

If you do nothing the keeper releases it after 5 seconds. Computer keepers pick from the same four and release it at once when the opponents are caught upfield.

## Runs and defending (computer players)

- **Third-man runs:** when you pass to a teammate, another attacker beyond them may set off in behind, timed to stay onside until the ball arrives. Play it on to them first time (through ball) as they go. Sometimes a forward makes a **decoy run** the other way to drag a defender out of the gap.
- Computer attackers time their runs on your back line too (a check towards the ball, then a sprint along the last defender's shoulder), and their defenders go with your runners and screen the passing lanes in front of your carrier (one lane on Pro, two on Elite and above).

## Your teammates (computer players on your side)

- **Sweeper keeper:** behind a high line your keeper stands higher, and comes off his line (out of the area if he has to) for a ball played in behind that he can reach first. Outside the area he plays it with his feet: a pass to a free teammate or a clearance.
- **Tracking runners:** with the ball in your last third, or their cross on its way, your defenders (and a holding midfielder close by) pick up every opponent in or running into your box and stay goal-side and ball-side of him.
- **Blocking shots:** with an opponent in shooting range, a teammate steps onto the line between the ball and goal; when the shot comes, teammates just off its path throw themselves in front of it (a block slide; no foul).
- **Second balls:** when a header, clearance or deflection is dropping, the teammate best placed attacks where it lands and others wait where it usually falls next, a few metres on and a few metres short.
- **Blindside runs:** while you carry the ball forward, your striker (or the far winger) sometimes drifts onto the far shoulder of a centre-back, out of his sight, then darts in behind on that side with an arm up. Computer defenders take a moment to pick the run up.
- **Underlaps:** when you are wide and the outside is blocked, your full-back runs inside you into the channel towards the corner of the area. With the ball in the middle and your winger holding the touchline, the full-back can also run the channel inside him.
- **Auto-switching** (Settings → Auto-switch) gives you the defender who will meet the next challenge first, goal-side: the receiver of their pass as it is played, where a high ball drops, or the dribbler's next stride. It switches sooner when the ball has gone past your man, and holds off while you are in the challenge or closing it down.
- **Partnerships:** teammates who keep passing to each other build an understanding during the match (a message says when a pair is *linking up*, and later has a *great understanding*). Their passes to each other are a little more accurate, the receiver reads them sooner, and the partner offers himself for the ball more. Pause → Player stats lists them.
- **On the ball for you:** when your keeper has the ball at his feet, or a restart is taken for you after the time limit, he looks for a free teammate going forward and clears it if anyone closes in, rather than dribbling into trouble.

## The score changes how the computer plays

From about the hour mark a computer side that is behind pushes its defensive line up, sends more players to press and run, goes forward more directly and shoots from further out; its keeper releases the ball at once. A side protecting a lead drops deeper, presses less, keeps the ball, runs it into the corner late on, and takes its time over goal kicks, throw-ins and the keeper's ball. The later it gets, the stronger the effect. Your own side is never changed.

## Rebounds and loose balls

Keepers sometimes spill a hard shot a few metres out instead of pushing it clear. After a shot, up to two attackers near the box follow it in for the rebound, and a loose ball in either box is attacked by two players from each side.

## Instant replay

Pause (Esc, Start or the pause button) and choose **Instant replay** to watch the last 20 seconds again. The bar along the bottom plays and pauses, jumps back or forward 5 seconds, changes the speed (1x, half, quarter) and the camera (match camera, net cam, ball cam, high cam). **Back** (B or Esc) returns to the pause menu. Offline matches only.

## Controller rumble

Where the browser supports it, the controller rumbles for your shots and tackles, fouls, goals and the woodwork. Turn it on or off in Settings (Gameplay and accessibility); the setting also says whether your controller and browser support it. Recent versions of Safari on iPad support it; older ones leave the controller still.

## Cup and league

**Cup and league** (main menu → More modes): choose a knockout cup (quarter-finals, semi-finals and the final; a draw goes to extra time and penalties) or a seven-round league (everyone plays everyone once). Eight clubs take part: Redmoor Rovers and Kestrel Bay plus Ironbridge Athletic, Northgate City, Saltmarsh United, Harrowfield Town, Vale Forge and Port Calder, and you can manage any of them. You play your own matches; the other results are worked out from the squads' strength. Progress is saved on the device, so you can stop and come back; **Abandon** (press twice) starts again.

## Player ratings and Man of the Match

At full time every player gets a rating from 3.0 to 10.0 for their goals, assists, chances created, passing, tackles, interceptions and saves (less for misplaced passes, fouls, cards and own goals; keepers and defenders answer for goals conceded). The best is named Man of the Match. **Player ratings** on the half-time and full-time screens switches between the team stats and the ratings.

## Touch controls for the new kicks

Chip: hold LOB and SHOOT together. Low drive: hold THROUGH and SHOOT together. The modifier (driven pass, lofted through ball, placed shot) has no touch button yet.

## Timed free kicks and penalties, and quick restarts

At a free kick or a penalty you take, a ring over the ball shrinks to a point and grows again (every 1.4 s). Release the kick as the ring closes on the small inner ring (it turns green): perfect timing roughly halves the usual error, and the worst timing doubles it. The computer's set pieces are unaffected.

Quick restarts (Settings → Quick restarts, on by default): press a kick button as your taker reaches the ball to take a throw-in, corner or free kick straight away, before the other side has set up. The stick picks the teammate, as in open play; there is no target and no timing ring. Penalties, goal kicks and kick-offs always wait.

Touch screens have no button for calling a run or rushing the keeper yet.

## Set pieces

When your team takes a free kick, corner or penalty, a yellow target appears. Move it with the stick or keys, then:

- **Free kick:** pass or lob goes to the target; shoot aims at the target's side of the goal. More shot power lifts the ball over the wall, too much clears the bar.
- **Corner:** lob crosses to the target; pass plays it short.
- **Penalty:** the target slides along the goal line only; hold and release shoot.

The computer picks its own target when it takes a set piece.

## Free kicks and penalties (20 Nov 2026)

**Takers and ratings.** Every player has a penalty (PEN), free-kick (FK) and nerve rating, built from his attributes. The pause menu and Match settings → More options have **Set-piece takers**: press Penalties, Free-kick shots or Corners to go through the players (best first), or All auto. With nobody chosen, the best penalty taker and free-kick specialist step up. A higher rating means a smaller error; under pressure (late in a close game, extra time, a shootout) a nervous taker's timing ring wobbles, and a computer taker's error grows. The restart hint shows the taker's rating, nerve and the pressure.

**Taking a penalty.** Aim along the line and time the ring as before, then:
- **Stutter:** press the skill button (U; R3 on a controller) before the kick. He checks in his run-up; a computer keeper may go early, and a composed taker then sends it the other way. A little less accurate.
- **Panenka:** chip (LOB and SHOOT). A soft dink down the middle: a goal if the keeper dives, a save if he stays.
- **Side-foot:** a placed shot (tap SWITCH while holding SHOOT). Slower, most accurate, aimed inside the post.
- **Blast:** a power shot (modifier + sprint + SHOOT). Fast and hard to save, but wilder.
- Keepers remember: after three penalties the same way the computer keeper leans that way (a toast warns you).

**Facing a penalty.** You are given your keeper. Move along the line with the stick (dancing on the line can put a nervous taker off), then point a way and press SHOOT to dive, or leave the stick centred and press SHOOT to stand tall. Commit too early and the taker sees it and goes the other way; save it after leaving your line too early and the referee orders a retake. The hint says which side the taker usually goes.

**Direct free kicks** (within about 30 m):
- **Run-up:** the skill button cycles standard, long (more pace, a little less accurate), short (tidier, softer) and straight-on (less curl). The taker moves to match.
- **Knuckleball:** modifier + sprint + SHOOT. No spin: it wobbles in the air and dips late. Harder for the keeper, harder to control.
- **Routines** (SWITCH before the kick): the free-kick list now also has **Two over the ball** (a second man runs over it first; a computer wall may jump at him) and **Attackers in the wall** (two of yours stand at the ends of their wall and peel off, unsighting the keeper).
- The referee paces out the ten yards and sprays a line in front of the wall and a mark by the ball. Waiting too long gives a computer wall time to creep forward: the referee pushes it back, and books one of them the second time.

**Defending a free kick:** as before, plus PASS puts a man lying down behind your wall against a shot under it. The computer sometimes does the same.

**Shootouts.** Both teams stand arm in arm on the halfway line; each taker walks up to the spot (press SHOOT to skip the walk), the crowd whistles at the away side's kicks, and the score bug counts down to sudden death.

**Set-piece camera** (Settings → Camera, on by default): for penalties and free kicks in shooting range the camera drops in behind the ball, looking at goal, then follows the shot. The stick works as you look in this view (up is towards goal). Off keeps your normal camera.

## Fouls, cards and advantage

Fouls can bring a yellow card; a second yellow or a straight red sends the player off, and the team plays on with ten. Denying an obvious goal-scoring chance with a foul outside the area is a red card. If the fouled side keeps the ball in a useful position, the referee may play advantage (an "ADVANTAGE" banner); if they lose it within 2.5 seconds the free kick is given where the foul happened. Sent-off players show as red cards on the score bar; booked players show a yellow marker on the player card.

## Visual cues

- Yellow triangle and ring: player 1's controlled player. Cyan diamond: player 2 (online).
- Faint white ring: who I/LB will switch to.
- White ring on a teammate: the intended receiver of a pass in flight.
- Yellow ring with a dot at a set piece: your target (see Set pieces).
- Yellow countdown ring round your keeper: time left to release the ball (six seconds).
- Settings offer high-contrast markers and can hide the next-switch ring.

## Graphics settings

Settings → Graphics. The preset changes the stadium and textures at the next kick-off; render scale and shadows apply at once.

| Preset | What it adds | Default on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | No antialiasing, no shadows, smaller textures, painted crowd, pixel ratio capped at 1 | Phones and tablets |
| Medium | Antialiasing, 1024 shadow map, 3D crowd on stepped terraces behind the goals and in the main stand, corner stands, pixel ratio up to 1.5 | |
| High | Fuller 3D crowd in every stand including the corners and the near side, 2048 shadow map that follows the ball, image-based lighting, light bloom, 4× multisampling, sharper grass with blade detail, double-resolution crowd, anisotropic filtering, pixel ratio up to 2 | Desktop |

## Practice

Main menu → More modes → Practice. There is no clock and no full time; use the pause menu to restart or quit.

- **Free play:** open play against the computer, for skill moves, called runs, barges and passing.
- **Free kicks:** a free kick in shooting range, from a new spot after each attempt. Time the ring for accuracy.
- **Penalties:** penalty after penalty against the keeper.
- **Crossing and finishing:** a winger crosses for your striker against two defenders and the keeper. Head it, or volley it as it drops.
- **Ball control drill** (60 seconds, a score to beat): dribble through the gate of two orange cones (100 points, up to 60 more for speed, 40 more for keeping the ball within a metre as you go through). Then pass to the coach (+20). He plays it back along the ground or in the air and the next gate goes up: a clean first touch scores 60 (90 off the chest or thigh), and 30 more when the touch already takes you towards the gate. Keepy-ups score 10 each; losing the ball costs 30. The best score is kept on this device.

**Challenges** (below the drills): ten attempts at free kicks, penalties or finishing, then your score. The best score for each is kept on this device and shown on its button.

## Added time, extra time and penalties

A minute before the end of each half the fourth official holds up a board with the added time (1 to 6 minutes: more for goals, fouls, cards and substitutions). The clock then reads 45:00 +m:ss. In Match settings, **If it is a draw** chooses what happens to a draw at 90 minutes: it stands (default), two halves of 15 minutes of extra time then penalties, or straight to penalties. In a shootout each side takes five, then sudden death; the score bug shows each kick (green scored, red missed). Online matches keep draws.

## Tactics and substitutions

Match settings and the pause menu hold your team instructions: formation (4-3-3, 4-4-2 or 4-2-3-1), three sliders, Width (narrow to wide), Defensive line (deep to high) and Pressing (stand off to press high), and **Defending corners and wide free kicks**: **Mixed** (default: zones at the near post, in front of the six-yard box and on the edge of the area, and everyone else marks a man), **Man-marking** (all but the near-post man and the edge pick up an attacker, goal-side) or **Zonal** (a line of zones across the six-yard box and the penalty spot, nobody follows a man). Markers and zones hold for a few seconds after the delivery. Changes in the pause menu apply at once, online too. The computer picks a formation at random each match.

Players tire over a match: their stamina bar stops recovering fully, and tired players lose their sprint sooner. Pause → Substitutions shows each player's bar and the bench (a keeper, defender, midfielder, winger and striker). Pick who comes off and who comes on; the change is made at the next stoppage. Five changes a match; a keeper can only be replaced by the bench keeper. The computer makes up to three changes from the hour mark.

## Pass assistance

Settings → Pass assistance. **Assisted** (default) picks the teammate and weighs the pass. **Semi** aims at a teammate near where you point (a narrower cone), but the hold alone sets how far the ball goes, so it can be under- or over-hit. **Manual** sends the ball exactly where you point, as far as the hold says.

## Difficulty

Casual, Pro and Elite are fixed. **Adaptive** starts at Elite and, every five minutes, sharpens up towards World class while you are ahead (goals and shots on target) or eases back towards Pro while you are behind. Where it finishes carries into your next match.

## Player instructions (pause menu)

Pause → **Player instructions** sets each outfield player to Stay back, Normal or Get forward for the rest of the match. Stay back: no forward runs, overlaps or box runs, sits deeper when you attack. Get forward: joins attacks, makes runs beyond the striker (midfielders and full-backs too) and stays up when you defend. **All normal** clears them. Teammates who are free and have a clear lane put an arm up and show a small "!" over their heads: they are calling for the ball.

## Auto-switching (20 Nov 2026)

Settings → Auto-switch: **Off**, **High balls** (only crosses and balls in the air), **Assisted** (high balls, and when the ball has gone past your man) or **Full** (default: whenever a clearly better-placed defender exists). **Auto-switch speed**: Calm, Normal (default) or Eager. Online, your choice is sent when you press Ready.

- It never switches you for a moment after you press tackle, slide or contain, or while you hold jockey or contain, or while you sprint at the ball.
- A better defender has to stay better for a quarter of a second, and the white next-switch ring stays on its man unless another is clearly better, so control doesn't flick about. A single switch press takes the man under the ring.
- After any switch while defending, the stick you were holding for the old player is ignored: the new man closes down the ball until you let go of the stick or push it a new way (at most 1.4 s).
- While an opponent winds up a pass, the ring moves to the defender nearest the receiver. If you are pressing the passer, you keep him until the ball is struck.
- Crosses and long balls coming down near your goal switch early to the defender who meets them.
- Players far up the pitch from the ball (probably off the screen) are picked only when clearly better. If your man is off the screen, a yellow arrow at the edge points to him, with his number.
- At their corners, free kicks and throw-ins near your goal you start on the defender nearest the danger (not one in the wall); switch picks another.
- **Switch flash and sound** (default On): your marker bursts out from the new man, with a soft click and, for automatic switches, a light buzz on a controller.
